A few good questions

we begin.

What do you do?

Two focused jobs: private browser trackers for enquiries, orders and follow-ups at CAD350, and client proposal preparation at CAD495. Both are scoped before payment. The cleaning proposal is one fictional example, not the only type of business we work with.

What is included in the CAD350 tracker?

One private browser tracker with agreed fields, up to 50 owner-provided initial records, local saves, backup export, a walkthrough and one revision. We agree the format, device, scope and delivery date first. It does not include automatic emails, cloud hosting or multi-device synchronisation.

Does the tracker send follow-up emails?

No. It helps you see what needs doing; you send your own messages. The public demo contains fictional examples and saves only in the browser where you use it. Exported backups matter because clearing browser data can remove records.

What do I need for a proposal?

Your client’s actual request, project notes, scope, schedule, approved prices and any evidence behind the claims you want included. We check what is missing before confirming the work.

Will you price or plan my service?

You supply and approve the price, scope and specialist approach. Grand Job handles writing and document preparation; it does not create a technical estimate or invent commercial terms for you.

Do you use AI?

Yes. AI assists with structure, drafting and formatting. The proposal is checked against the information you provide, and you confirm the factual and technical content before the release version. Missing qualifications, evidence or terms are flagged rather than invented.

Can you handle a government tender?

Formal tenders, public procurement submissions, legal contracts and mandatory compliance certification are outside this introductory pilot.

Does a proposal guarantee the contract?

No. The prospect’s decision depends on factors including your price, experience and service. You are paying for the agreed proposal preparation, not a promised sales result.

What should I put in the first message?

A short description is enough to check fit. Leave out passwords, financial account details, health information and unnecessary personal data. You can use fictional examples in the initial brief; any required business files are agreed separately.
