If you sell to other businesses

The new-business list

Every Monday, a list of the businesses newly licensed in Calgary: trading name, address, sector and contact details. Sign makers, insurers, cleaners, printers, point-of-sale dealers and equipment suppliers are all chasing the same customers, and most hear about them far too late.

Each list is checked by hand before it goes out. Licence renewals, relocations and second premises of established companies are removed, so what arrives on Monday is genuinely new.

I sell to one business per trade, so the list you receive is not going to your direct competitor.

Terms

Terms

Terms of engagement

The commercial terms that apply to every project, stated plainly.

Approval before payment

You see the completed work before it goes live and before an invoice is issued. Revisions are included until it meets your requirements. If it does not, no payment is due.

What I need from you

A phone call and your photographs. I handle everything technical. Where a job needs access to an account you already own — your Google listing, for instance — you grant that access yourself and I talk you through it, usually in under ten minutes. I will never ask you for a password.

No contract

One-off work carries no ongoing commitment. Monthly services can be stopped at any time by sending me a message, ending from the following month.

You own the work

The website, the domain name and the listings are yours, in your name. All access is handed over to you on completion, and moving to another provider is straightforward at any point.

Fixed pricing

The prices on this page are the prices charged. There are no hourly rates and no variable charges. Anything outside the list is quoted in writing first.

About

About Grand Job

Grand Job is run by Jason Daly — from Cork, based in Calgary. Every project is done personally: the person you speak to is the person doing the work.

My background is in regulated clinical-trial documentation, where accuracy is audited and mistakes are not tolerated. That is the standard I work to here.

Most businesses I deal with are well run and simply let down by something small online — a menu that cannot be read on a phone, opening hours wrong on Google for years, a website that quietly stopped working. That says nothing about the business. It says how busy the people running it are.
